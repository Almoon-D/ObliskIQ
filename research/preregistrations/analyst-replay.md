# The analyst's segments replayed after their models' training cutoff (H15)

A replay: fact sheets rebuilt as they stood on past dates the models could not know, the calls
the analyst's segments write on them, graded at once on prices that already exist. This file
fixes, before any replay call is made, which models and segments are replayed, on which dates,
from which sections, how a call is graded, how memory is told from judgement, what the power is,
and the one thing a verdict may change. Not investment advice.

## Mechanism and citation

The forward ledger (H14) needs about two years a segment for a verdict (`idea-ledger.md`, Power).
A model's training cutoff gives a shortcut (DECISIONS D191): on a date after it the model knows
no more than the sheet tells it, so a sheet rebuilt as of that date asks for the call it would
have written then, and the market has already graded it.

The evidence for an edge is thin. Lopez-Lira and Tang (2023) found that a model's reading of a
day's headlines predicted the next day's return; this replay cannot rebuild news as it stood
(below), so their channel is closed here. No study shows a model picking stocks from prices,
filings and macro data beyond chance out of sample, and few active managers show skill beyond
luck (Fama and French 2010). The replay is run because a reading in days chooses which segment
the forward record keeps and which the owner is offered as the one that trades, not because an
edge is likely. It moves no capital.

The risk that decides how it is read is **memory**: a model whose training reached a date
recalls which names rose after it (Glasserman and Lin 2023; Sarkar and Vafa 2024).

## The models and their cutoffs

A model is replayed only on a **dated snapshot id whose provider documents its training
cutoff**, read on the provider's own pages and dated (`cutoff_read`). Read on 2026-11-27:

- **Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite** and **Gemini 3.8 Flash.** Google's model pages state no cutoff of
  their own and call each id *stable* ("usually don't change"), not frozen. Each model card names
  another card for its training dataset; the chain ends at Gemini 3 Pro's, which states a
  knowledge cutoff of **January 2025**. The cutoff is therefore **inherited** (`cutoff_source`),
  not stated for the model itself, and both models were updated later (May and September 2026).
  This registration accepts the inherited cutoff, says so beside every result, and adds the
  memory probe below to test it. An owner who rules otherwise rules a new registration.
- **Claude Fable 5.2**: a pinned snapshot with a stated training cutoff of June 2026, which
  leaves about thirteen weekly sheets. No Anthropic key is in the environment, so the segment
  `strong` (Flash-Lite scans, Fable writes) is **not replayed** here (`no_key`).

A model with no documented cutoff, or an alias that may be refreshed (a `-latest` or preview
id), is `no_cutoff` and is never replayed; the command refuses it.

## The segments

The forward segments whose models all qualify (Session 33, DECISIONS D198): **`lite`** (Flash-Lite
scans and writes) and **`mid`** (Flash-Lite scans, Flash writes). A segment's cutoff is the later
of its models'. Each is replayed with the forward prompt, schemas, temperature (0), retries and
caps, on the replay sheet below; the replay sheet's shape enters the segment's hash, so a
**replay segment is never the forward one**. Tools and search are off.

## The dates

The cutoff month's last day is the **cutoff day**: 2025-01-31. The **margin** is 31 days: a
month's text can be published, crawled and trained on within weeks, and a cutoff stated by the
month is uncertain by up to a month. Sheets are Mondays.

- **After the cutoff**: every Monday from **2025-03-03** (the first Monday at least 31 days after
  the cutoff day) to **2026-10-19** (the last whose 20-session horizon closed before this
  registration): **86 sheets**.
- **Before the cutoff, the leak control**: the same 86 Mondays ending **2024-11-25** (the last
  Monday at least 31 days before the cutoff month's first day), from **2023-04-10**.
- A sheet date between those two windows is **inside the margin** and is refused.

## The sheet

A replay sheet is the forward builder's (`grid/facts.py`) read on a store filled by the replay's
loaders and bounded by the sheet date, every row stamped with the time its source published it:

- **instrument**: the universe instruments' closes and returns, a close known after its session;
- **stock_screen**: the untested screen (D180), its fundamentals each dated by the filing the
  SEC's company facts give it, its closes after their session.

Omitted, and the sheet says so on one line: **macro**, **grid** and **ranking** (built by the
compute step from stored measurements; rebuilding them as of each past date means the macro
series' vintages and the whole compute chain run 172 times, more machinery than one reading
needs), **positioning**, **market**, **pulse**
and **news** (stored only since first collected: no free source carries their past as it stood),
and **insider** (the SEC dates each Form 4, but rebuilding four years of them is every filing's
document fetched at the SEC's fair-access pace, days of work for one section: least machinery).

## The universe and what is not evaluable

At each sheet date the replay universe is the companies the SEC's full index shows filing a 10-K
or 10-Q in the fifteen months before it, with the ticker the SEC's records give, whatever became
of them since. A name with no free close over its horizon (most that delisted: Yahoo no longer
serves them) is **not evaluable**: counted per cohort with its reason, never dropped in silence.
A cohort whose not-evaluable share of its universe passes **0.35** is not evaluable whole: its
equal-weight benchmark would be of survivors.

## The outcome of a call

A **call** is a valid draft of the dossier pass on a sheet, its fact references checked against
that sheet. From the **first close after the sheet date** (`P₀`) to the close 20 sessions later
(`P₁`), with `B` the equally weighted gross return of the cohort's evaluable universe names over
the same sessions:

    e = ln(P₁ / P₀) − ln(B) − c

where `c` is the round trip's cost as H14 states it (the cost model's two legs on a trade of
10,000 in the base currency). A call whose instrument has no close within 7 days of either end,
or a gap of more than 7 days between, is not evaluable, counted. Beside it, never decisive: the
same return over the **equity reference** (H14's `world_equity` role), and the call **at its own
levels** as H14's grader resolves it (target first, invalidation first or horizon), where it
resolved by the prices' end.

## Primary statistic and the verdict (D62)

On **stock calls** (a call on a universe instrument is graded only beside, against the equity
reference). The cohort of a call is its sheet's week. A segment's estimate is the mean of its
cohorts' mean `e`, with a Newey–West standard error of **4 lags** (20 sessions span four weekly
cohorts), floored at the independent variance; its effective observations are H14's
`N_eff = Σ n_c / (1 + (n_c − 1) ρ) / 4` with ρ = 0.25. The interval `[L, U]` is two-sided at
level `1 − 2α/k`, α = 0.05, `k` the number of segments past the minimum (Bonferroni).

**Minimum**: 26 evaluable cohorts and N_eff ≥ 30; below it the segment is "too few".

**Verdict** against θ = **0.005** a call (half a percent over 20 sessions after costs, about six
percent a year): sufficient when `L ≥ θ`; insufficient when `U < θ` (negative when `U < 0`);
inconclusive otherwise. The verdict that counts is the one **after the cutoff**, on the weeks the
probe leaves.

## Theta and why

θ = 0.005 a call over 20 sessions. A segment that cannot beat the names it could have picked by
half a percent a month after costs is not worth a slot or a share of the budget; the forward
ledger's θ (0.01 over about 13 weeks) is lower a month, and the replay is the shorter, noisier
read.

## Memory: the leak measure and the probe

**The leak measure**: a segment's estimate before the cutoff minus after it, with the interval
of the difference at the same level. A segment that is sufficient before the cutoff and not after
is reported as **remembering**, and its verdict is the one after.

**The probe**, run before any replay call, for each model: in each calendar quarter of both
windows, 40 questions (seed 15) each drawn as one evaluable universe name and one sheet date of
that quarter, asking with nothing but the ticker, the company's name and the date for the
probability that the name closed higher 20 sessions later. A quarter is **recalled** when the
model's hits (a probability above one half where the close rose, below where it fell) beat the
probe sample's majority outcome on a one-sided binomial test at 0.05. A recalled quarter after
the cutoff is **trimmed** from the primary statistic, which is reported with and without it. The
quarters before the cutoff are the control: a model that recalls none of them shows the probe
has no power to see its memory, and the report says so.

## Calibration

Each segment's stated probabilities (target first) on its calls resolved at their own levels by
the prices' end: the Brier score beside the segment's climatology, a reliability table in fifths,
read past 26 cohorts and N_eff ≥ 30 (H14's rule, with the replay's minimum). Calls with long
horizons resolve less often by the prices' end, so the resolved calls lean to quick outcomes: the
report says how many resolved.

## Power

Stated before any call. A stock call's excess over 20 sessions has a standard deviation near
0.10 (a single name's month, beside the scoreboard's 0.06 for funds and stocks over 28 days,
`scoreboard.md`). At five calls a cohort, 86 cohorts give N_eff ≈ 54 a segment after the cutoff:

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Standard error a call | 0.0136 |
| Excess found with probability 0.5 (`L ≥ θ`, two segments) | 0.032 |
| Excess found with probability 0.8 | 0.043 |
| Standard error of the leak measure | 0.019 |

**No segment can detect the registered effect**: the sample sees an edge of about three percent
a month, six times θ, and fewer calls a cohort see less. The excess statistic is computed and
reported with its verdict, but what this registration may decide rests on the **calibration and
leak readings** (below): a large memory effect is visible, a skill of θ is not. D154's floor does
not apply: the replay funds nothing (D191).

## What a verdict may change

Only two things, both proposed to the owner, neither automatic:

- **The forward budget shares**: a segment found remembering, or negative after the cutoff
  (`U < 0`), is proposed to lose its share of the forward budget; the others keep theirs.
- **The trading segment**: the one proposed to the owner as `analyst.trading_segment` (his
  setting, audited, D177) is a segment not remembering whose after-cutoff verdict is sufficient;
  failing one, a segment not remembering whose calibration is read and shows Brier skill above
  zero; failing that, none, and `lite` stays.

The replay record (`replay`) is never pooled with shadow, paper or live, never read by H13 or
H14, and never reaches capital, a size, a slot or the gate. A new prompt after the verdict is a
new segment and needs a new registration.

## The cost

The most the replay may spend is `llm.replay.max_usd` in `config/llm.yaml`, the owner's setting.
The command refuses a run whose worst case would pass it, and is resumable: a stopped run repeats
no paid call.

## Robustness checks

Reported beside, never decisive: the statistic untrimmed by the probe; over the equity reference;
at the calls' own levels; the record without its best cohort; the not-evaluable counts per cohort
and their reasons; each model's probe hits per quarter.

## Trial budget

Zero. There is no development sample: the prompts are the forward ones, fixed before this file,
and no replay call exists at its merge.

## What each outcome leads to (D62)

- **Sufficient after the cutoff and not remembering**: the segment is proposed as the trading
  segment; the owner decides.
- **Inconclusive or too few**: the forward ledger keeps reading it; nothing changes.
- **Remembering, or negative after the cutoff**: proposed to lose its forward budget share.
- **Calibration read with skill above zero**: the segment may be proposed when none is
  sufficient.
- **Not evaluable**: counted with why; the missing prices are data work.
